Royalties & Money Flows
Performance Royalty Royalties

Money earned when your music is performed publicly — radio, TV, live venues, streaming, bars, restaurants, gyms, and more.

These royalties are paid to songwriters and publishers, not the master owner. They are tracked and collected by Performance Rights Organizations (PROs).

Collected by:

ASCAP BMI SESAC
Songwriter Publisher PRO
Mechanical Royalty Royalties

Money earned when your composition is reproduced — streaming, downloads, CDs, vinyl, ringtones, and more.

In the U.S., digital mechanicals from interactive streaming services are collected and paid out by The Mechanical Licensing Collective (The MLC).

The MLC
Composition Streaming Mechanical
Neighboring Rights Royalties

Royalties paid to performers and master owners when sound recordings are played publicly in certain territories (radio, TV, public venues).

In the U.S., digital performance royalties for sound recordings (like Pandora, SiriusXM) are collected by SoundExchange.

SoundExchange
Master Owner Performer Digital Radio
Sync (Synchronization) License Royalties

A license that allows your music to be used with visual media — film, TV, ads, games, trailers, YouTube, etc.

Sync deals usually involve an upfront fee and may also generate performance royalties when the content is broadcast.

Manage Sync Opportunities

Advance Business

Money paid to you upfront by a label, publisher, or distributor, usually recoupable from your future royalties.

An advance is not free money — it’s a loan against your future income. You may not see additional royalties until the advance is fully recouped.

Rights & Ownership
Copyright Rights

Legal protection for original works of authorship — including songs, lyrics, recordings, artwork, and more.

In many countries, copyright exists automatically when the work is created, but registration strengthens your legal position and your ability to enforce your rights.

U.S. Copyright Office
Legal Registration Ownership
Master Rights Rights

Ownership of the actual sound recording — the final audio file. Whoever owns the master controls how the recording is used and licensed.

Labels often own masters in traditional deals. In independent setups, the artist or their company may own the masters.

Sound Recording Label Ownership
Publishing Rights Rights

Rights tied to the underlying composition — melody, lyrics, and structure of the song.

Publishers help register works, collect royalties, and enforce your rights. You can self‑publish or sign with a publisher.

Publishing Tools
Songwriting PRO Publisher
Work for Hire Contracts

A legal arrangement where you are paid to create something, but the person or company paying you owns the copyright from the start.

Common in production, custom compositions, and some session work. Read these clauses carefully — you may be giving up ownership.

Codes, IDs & Registrations
ISRC (International Standard Recording Code) Codes & IDs

A unique identifier for each sound recording. Think of it as a serial number for the master.

ISRCs help track plays, sales, and royalties for specific recordings across platforms.

Official ISRC Site
Master Tracking Metadata
ISWC (International Standard Musical Work Code) Codes & IDs

A unique identifier for the composition (the song itself), separate from any specific recording.

Used by PROs and publishers to track and pay royalties for the underlying work.

Composition PRO Metadata
IPI / CAE Number Codes & IDs

A unique number assigned to songwriters and publishers by PROs. It identifies you as a rights holder worldwide.

You’ll see this on your PRO account (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, etc.).

Check PRO Setup

UPC / EAN Codes & IDs

Barcodes used to identify releases (albums, singles, EPs) in stores and digital platforms.

Distributors often assign these when you upload your release.

Contracts & Deal Types
Split Sheet Contracts

A document listing all contributors to a song and their ownership percentages for the composition.

This should be filled out as soon as the song is finished — before anyone forgets who did what.

Split Sheet Template
Songwriting Ownership Collaboration
360 Deal Contracts

A record deal where the label takes a percentage of multiple income streams — not just recorded music, but also touring, merch, brand deals, and more.

These deals can provide big support but can also be very expensive in terms of control and revenue share.

Label Touring Merch
Distribution Deal Contracts

An agreement where a distributor gets your music onto platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, etc.) and pays you a share of revenue.

Some distributors are flat‑fee, some take a percentage, some offer label‑style services.

DSPs Revenue Share Indie
Producer Agreement Contracts

A contract that defines how a producer is paid (fee, points/percentage, or both) and what rights they have in the master and composition.

Often includes ownership splits, recoupment terms, and credit language.

Management Agreement Contracts

A contract between an artist and a manager that defines responsibilities, commission percentage, and length of the relationship.

Managers typically earn a percentage of the artist’s gross or net income.

Recoupment Business

The process where a label, publisher, or company recovers money they advanced to you from your future royalties.

Until recoupment is complete, you may not see additional royalty payments.

Advance Royalties Deals
Backend / Points Business

“Points” usually refer to a percentage of revenue from a master recording (e.g., a producer getting 3 points = 3% of certain income).

Always read how “points” are defined in the contract — gross vs net, which income streams, and after what recoupment.

Producer Master Royalties
Territory Business

The geographic area where a contract applies (e.g., “World,” “North America,” “EU”).

A deal limited to one territory may leave you free to sign other deals elsewhere.

Contracts Licensing Global
Term Business

How long a contract lasts. Can be a fixed number of years, album cycles, or options controlled by the label/publisher.

Term + territory + rights = the core of what you’re giving up or sharing.
