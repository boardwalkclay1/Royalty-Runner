Learn the Language

This glossary is here so you never have to pretend you understand something. If a word feels confusing anywhere in Royalty Runner, you can come here and get a clear, honest explanation.

Key Terms

Performance Royalty

Money earned when your music is performed publicly—on radio, TV, live venues, or streaming services. Collected by PROs like ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, PRS, SOCAN.

Mechanical Royalty

Money earned when your music is reproduced—streaming, downloads, CDs, vinyl. Collected by mechanical agencies like The MLC or Harry Fox, depending on your region.

Publishing

The business side of your songwriting and composition rights. A publisher or administrator helps register your works and collect royalties worldwide.

Neighboring Rights

Royalties paid to performers and master owners when recordings are played publicly in certain territories. Organizations like SoundExchange and PPL handle these.

PRO (Performance Rights Organization)

An organization that collects performance royalties for songwriters and publishers. Examples: ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, PRS, SOCAN.

ISRC

International Standard Recording Code. A unique ID for each sound recording. Used to track plays and royalties for the master.

ISWC

International Standard Musical Work Code. A unique ID for the composition (songwriting).

Split Sheet

A document that lists all contributors to a song and their percentage of ownership. This protects everyone and prevents fights later.

Work‑for‑Hire

An agreement where someone is paid to create something and does not own the rights. The person or company paying owns the final work.

Trademark

Legal protection for names, logos, and brand identifiers. Used to protect your artist name, logo, and brand from being used by others in confusing ways.

Copyright

Legal protection for original works of authorship, like songs and recordings. Registration strengthens your rights and your ability to enforce them.
