Protect Your Ownership

This page gives you everything you need to legally protect your music, your brand, your identity, and your creative work. If any term feels confusing, the Royalty Runner Glossary teaches you everything you need to know.

1. Copyright Basics

Copyright protects your music, lyrics, recordings, artwork, videos, and creative ideas. You own copyright the moment you create something — but registration gives you:

  • Legal proof you created it
  • The right to sue for infringement
  • Eligibility for statutory damages
  • Public record of ownership

What you can register:

  • Song (composition)
  • Sound recording (master)
  • Album or collection
  • Artwork / logos
  • Videos

Direct links:

  • US Copyright Office – Register a Work
  • USCO – Search Public Records
  • USCO – Group Registration for Songs

Want to see all royalty organizations? Visit the Royalty Catalog A–Z.

2. Types of Copyright

Composition (Songwriting)

Protects the lyrics and melody. This is tied to your PRO (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, etc.).

Sound Recording (Master)

Protects the actual audio file. This is tied to ISRCs, distributors, and SoundExchange.

Artwork & Visuals

Album covers, logos, photos, and branding can all be copyrighted.

Video Content

Music videos, lyric videos, and visualizers can be registered as audiovisual works.

Definitions for all of these live in the Glossary.

3. Trademarks – Protect Your Name & Brand

A trademark protects your artist name, logo, slogan, and brand identity. This prevents others from using confusingly similar names in music or merch.

Why trademarks matter:

  • Stops others from using your name
  • Protects your merch and branding
  • Gives you legal ownership of your identity
  • Helps with social media verification

Direct links:

  • USPTO – Trademark Search (TESS)
  • USPTO – File a Trademark
  • USPTO – Trademark Classes (Music & Merch)

Need help understanding trademark classes? The Glossary breaks them down.

4. Copyright vs Trademark

These two protections work together:

  • Copyright protects your creative work (songs, recordings, artwork).
  • Trademark protects your identity (name, logo, brand).

Example: Your song is copyrighted. Your artist name is trademarked.

5. How to Register Your Work

Step 1 — Prepare Your Files

  • Final audio file (WAV/MP3)
  • Lyrics (if registering composition)
  • Artwork (if registering visuals)
  • Split sheet (optional but recommended)

Step 2 — Choose Your Registration Type

  • Single work
  • Group of songs
  • Sound recording
  • Composition
  • Artwork

Step 3 — File Online

Use the USCO portal (or your country’s equivalent). Links above.

Step 4 — Save Your Receipt
