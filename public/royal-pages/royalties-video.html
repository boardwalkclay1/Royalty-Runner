Video Platforms & Visual Royalties

How Video Royalties Work

Video platforms pay through ads, subscriptions, and licensing. Your music can earn money when it is:

  • In a music video
  • In a vlog, gaming video, or tutorial
  • In a film, short, or documentary
  • In user-generated content (UGC)

Money flows through ad revenue shares, Content ID, sync licenses, and performance royalties.

Major Video Platforms

YouTube (Main Platform)

YouTube

Pays: Ad revenue (YouTube Partner Program), YouTube Premium, Content ID.

Music angle: Music videos, lyric videos, visualizers, live sessions.

YouTube Content ID

Content ID

Pays: Claims on other people’s videos that use your music.

Access: Via distributor, label, or specialized Content ID partner.

YouTube Shorts

Shorts

Pays: Revenue share from Shorts ad pool + music usage.

Vimeo

Vimeo

Pays: Via Vimeo On Demand, rentals, and direct sales.

Dailymotion

Dailymotion

Pays: Ad revenue share for partners.

Bilibili (China)

Bilibili

Pays: Creator monetization + indirect music exposure.

Live & Long-Form Video

Twitch (Video + Live)

Twitch

Pays: Subs, Bits, ads; performance royalties via PROs.

Music angle: Live sets, concerts, studio streams.

Facebook Watch

Facebook Watch

Pays: Ad revenue share for eligible creators.

Instagram Video

Instagram

Pays: Brand deals, bonuses, and music usage via Meta’s licenses.

Film, TV & Long-Form Platforms (Indirect)

Netflix

Netflix

Pays: Through sync licenses + performance royalties (via PROs).

Hulu / Disney+ / Prime Video / HBO Max

All major streaming services pay via sync fees + performance royalties.

Independent Film Platforms

Filmhub, Vimeo On Demand, and indie platforms pay via rentals, purchases, and sync.
