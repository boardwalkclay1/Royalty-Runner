Step 1

Register Your Songs – Publishing & PROs

Your PRO is the engine that tracks performances of your songs on radio, TV, live shows, and streaming. If your songs aren’t registered, those performances can’t be matched to you.

  • Choose your PRO (ASCAP, BMI, SESAC, PRS, SOCAN, etc.).
  • Register each song with correct writers, splits, and publishers.
  • Match titles exactly with your distributor and copyright filings.

Tip: Use the same spelling, punctuation, and capitalization for titles everywhere.

  • ASCAP – Writer/Publisher Registration
  • BMI – Song Registration
  • SESAC / PRS / SOCAN – Local PRO Portals

Step 2

Mechanical & Digital Royalties

Streaming and downloads generate mechanical royalties. If your songs aren’t connected to the right mechanical agency, money can sit in limbo instead of reaching you.

  • Make sure every released song is registered with the mechanical agency in your region.
  • Confirm your distributor has correct songwriter and publisher info.

Think of this as the “back‑end plumbing” that makes sure your streams actually pay out.

Royal Links:

  • The MLC – US Mechanical Royalties
  • Harry Fox Agency

Step 3

Copyright Registration

Copyright exists the moment you create the work, but registration gives you stronger legal power if someone steals or misuses your music. It’s your official timestamp.

  • Register your songs and/or sound recordings with your country’s copyright office.
  • Group songs into collections when allowed to save on fees.
  • Keep your titles and credits consistent across all platforms.

If you ever need to enforce your rights, a registration number is your best friend.

Step 4

Trademarks & Brand Protection

Your artist name, logo, and brand identity can be protected with trademarks so others can’t legally use confusingly similar names in your lane.

  • Search to see if your artist name is already in use.
  • Consider filing a trademark for your name and logo in key classes (music, merch, etc.).
  • Keep records of when you started using the name publicly.

Your name is an asset. Treat it like one.

Royal Links:

  • USPTO – Trademark Search
  • USPTO – Trademark Application

Step 5

Neighboring Rights & Master Royalties

When your recordings are played on radio, TV, and in some territories overseas, neighboring rights organizations collect money for performers and master owners.

  • Register as a featured performer and/or rights owner.
  • Make sure your ISRCs and track data are correct.

This is where your recording (the master) earns, separate from the song itself.

Royal Links:

  • SoundExchange – US Digital Performance
  • PPL – UK
  • Other CMOs in your region

Step 6

Business, Splits & Agreements

Protection isn’t just legal filings—it’s also clear agreements with the people you work with. The more you put in writing, the less you leave to memory and emotion.

  • Use split sheets for every song to lock in percentages.
  • Use work‑for‑hire agreements when you pay others to create for you.
  • Consider forming an LLC or company for your music business.

Things to keep on file:

  • Signed split sheets (PDFs, photos, or scans).
  • Work‑for‑hire agreements.
  • Operating agreement / LLC documents.

Your Protection Checklist

Use this as your personal scoreboard. Revisit it every few months as your catalog grows.

  • [ ] I chose a PRO and registered as a writer.
  • [ ] I registered my songs with my PRO.
  • [ ] My songs are registered with the mechanical agency (like The MLC).
  • [ ] I registered key works with the copyright office.
  • [ ] I searched my artist name and considered a trademark.
  • [ ] I registered with SoundExchange / neighboring rights orgs.
  • [ ] I use split sheets and keep copies.
  • [ ] I have a basic business structure (like an LLC).
